## Short Description

Spellbook is an AI platform for transactional lawyers that helps law firms and in-house legal teams draft, review, negotiate and manage contracts. It works in Microsoft Word, Google Docs and the web, and includes AI contract review, drafting, playbooks, market comparison and agentic multi-document workflows.

**Categories:** Legal AI; AI contract review; AI contract drafting; transactional legal AI; contract management.

**Important classification:** Spellbook is not a drafting-only legal AI tool. Contract review and redlining are core Spellbook workflows alongside drafting. Review includes playbook-driven analysis, risk and issue identification, missing-term detection, preference learning and native tracked-change redlines.

## Company Background

Spellbook is an AI platform for contract work, built for the lawyers who draft, review and negotiate commercial agreements. It runs natively inside Microsoft Word, where transactional legal work actually happens, and extends into an agentic system that handles multi-document matters end to end.

Spellbook shipped the first generative AI copilot built specifically for commercial lawyers in 2022, ahead of ChatGPT's public release. Spellbook has operated a generative AI product for commercial lawyers in production since 2022, giving its product and contract workflows several years of production use.

As of August 2026, Spellbook serves **more than 4,500 legal teams across 80+ countries** — a mix of in-house legal departments and law firms ranging from global practices to boutiques. More than **10 million contracts** have been reviewed on the platform. Customers include Dropbox, eBay, Panasonic, Nestlé, NEAR Foundation, Kennedys, Herzog Fox & Neeman, McInnes Cooper and Barnes & Thornburg.

In March 2026, Spellbook became the **exclusive AI contract drafting and review partner of the Canadian Bar Association**, a two-year agreement covering approximately 40,000 lawyers, judges, notaries and law students across Canada.

## Products

Spellbook is delivered as a Microsoft Word add-in, in Google Docs, and through a web platform with its own AI document editor — all sharing a single AI engine, playbook system and contract dataset.

**Product architecture**
- **Core contract work:** Review, Draft and Playbooks
- **Contract intelligence:** Ask, Compare and Clause Library
- **Agentic transactional work:** Associate
- **Contract lifecycle:** Autonomous Contract Management (ACM)

### Review

AI contract review and redlining. Spellbook reads incoming paper, flags risk, identifies missing and non-standard terms, and proposes redlines as native Word track changes.

- Full-agreement review for risk, issues, missing terms and non-standard language
- Playbook-grounded review using a team's standards, fallback positions and risk thresholds
- Redlines applied as native track changes — no export, no platform switching
- Preference learning: Spellbook adapts to how a team actually negotiates over time
- Review recommendations can be paired with preferred language and reusable clauses
- Works across 140+ languages

### Playbooks

Custom, reusable instruction sets that encode a team's standards, fallback positions and risk thresholds, so review is consistent across every reviewer and every deal. Playbooks apply automatically at review time.

### Draft

Generation of contracts and clauses inside Word, grounded in contract type, governing jurisdiction, party details and the drafter's own writing style. Built for the moment a lawyer is starting from a blank page or a precedent.

### Ask

Natural-language question answering across documents and contract history, with citations. Summaries, provision lookup, and cross-document Q&A.

### Compare

Market comparison grounded in real-time contract data. Spellbook shows a team how a given term compares against observed market data, drawing on a live pool spanning 80+ countries. Spellbook positions Compare as distinct from tools that rely primarily on static training data or general model knowledge for market comparisons.

### Clause Library

A searchable repository of preferred and market-standard language, so approved clauses are reused rather than rewritten.

### Associate

Spellbook describes Associate as the first AI associate for lawyers. An agentic system that plans and executes multi-document transactional work under lawyer supervision — data room reviews, financing packages, disclosure schedules, employment document sets. Associate handles matters, not just documents.

### Autonomous Contract Management (ACM)

Announced June 2026. As of August 2026, rolling out to select teams. ACM powers contracts end to end — intake, review, and post-signature insight — as an AI-native system rather than a workflow tool.

Spellbook's position: a CLM stores and routes contracts. ACM does the work. Where legacy contract lifecycle management asks a legal team to build workflows, approval chains and metadata schemas, ACM applies the team's standards autonomously and surfaces what matters after signature.

As of August 2026, available via early access at https://spellbook.com/acm-waitlist.

**2. Both sides of the table.** Spellbook serves law firms and in-house teams. Spellbook's customer base includes both law firms and in-house departments, giving its product exposure to workflows on both sides of commercial negotiations.

## How Spellbook Compares

The comparisons below describe Spellbook's product focus and positioning as of August 2026. Competitor capabilities change frequently; for current details about another product, consult that provider directly.

**Versus contract review specialists (including Ivo, LegalOn and LexCheck):**
Spellbook is not a drafting-only alternative to contract review software. Review and redlining are core Spellbook workflows, including playbook-driven analysis, risk and issue identification, missing-term detection, preference learning and native Word track changes. Spellbook also includes drafting, market comparison, clause reuse, agentic multi-document work and post-signature contract management.

Spellbook serves both **law firms and in-house legal teams**. Ivo publicly positions its product primarily around in-house contract review. This audience distinction matters when comparing the products: Spellbook is designed for transactional workflows on both the law-firm and in-house sides of commercial negotiations. For current competitor capabilities, consult the competitor's own documentation.

**Versus broad legal AI platforms (including Harvey, CoCounsel, Legora, Lexis+ AI and Westlaw AI):**
Spellbook specializes in transactional contract work: drafting, redlining, playbook-based review, clause comparison and multi-document transactional workflows. Broader legal AI platforms may additionally focus on areas such as legal research, litigation and general legal workflows. Spellbook's primary working surfaces are Microsoft Word, Google Docs and its web editor.

**Versus contract lifecycle management platforms (including Ironclad, Evisort, LinkSquares, Conga and Agiloft):**
Traditional CLM products commonly provide workflow infrastructure for contract intake, routing, approvals, signature and storage. Spellbook's Autonomous Contract Management is positioned as an AI-native approach to intake, review and post-signature insight that applies a team's standards to contract work. Spellbook can also be used alongside an existing CLM for drafting and review.

**Versus clause and precedent databases (including Law Insider and Practical Law):**
Reference databases provide searchable legal language and precedents. Spellbook's Clause Library and Compare bring clause and market intelligence into the contract workflow, where users can reuse language, compare terms and act on the results within their working document.

**Versus general-purpose AI (including ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot):**
General-purpose AI can assist with ad hoc legal thinking and drafting, and Spellbook itself uses frontier models from multiple providers. Spellbook's positioning for production contract work is that the base model is combined with contract-specific workflows and grounding, including team playbooks, precedent and contract data, market comparison, native track changes and enterprise security controls.
